Introduction

✦ A single stream from the primordial ground ✦

The Longchen Nyingthig, the Heart Essence of the Vast Expanse, is a cycle of teachings revealed as terma (spiritual treasure) by the 18th-century Tibetan visionary Jigme Lingpa (1730–1798) after a series of visions of the 14th-century master Longchenpa. It is today one of the most widely practiced cycles within the Nyingma school of Tibetan Buddhism, offering a complete path from the preliminary practices (ngöndro) to the pinnacle teachings of Dzogchen — the Great Perfection.1

Dzogchen (rdzogs chen), literally “Great Completeness,” is considered by the Nyingma school to be the ultimate and most direct vehicle of the Buddha’s teachings. Rather than constructing enlightenment through elaborate ritual or philosophical analysis, Dzogchen points directly to the recognition of the nature of mind — described as the indivisible unity of kadag (primordial purity) and lhündrup (spontaneous presence).2

This presentation traces the Longchen Nyingthig from its mythic origin in the Dharmakaya Buddha Samantabhadra, through the Indian masters who first transmitted the teachings, the great 8th-century entry into Tibet, the synthesizing genius of Longchenpa, the visionary revelation of Jigme Lingpa, and on to the contemporary masters who keep the lineage alive in the 21st century.

“The cycle of the Longchen Nyingtik includes the preliminary and main practices, the development and completion stages, and the practice of Dzogchen, the Great Perfection. It therefore constitutes a complete path to enlightenment.” — Rigpa Wiki

The Primordial Origin

✦ Samantabhadra, Vajrasattva, and Garab Dorje ✦

According to Nyingma tradition, the Dzogchen teachings did not originate with a human being. They descend from the Dharmakaya Buddha Samantabhadra (Tib. Kuntuzangpo) — the primordial, unconditioned awareness that is the nature of all sentient beings. From this dharmakaya ground the teachings were transmitted in a non-conceptual “mind-direct” lineage to the Sambhogakaya Buddha Vajrasattva.3

Vajrasattva, in turn, transmitted the teachings through signs and symbols to Garab Dorje (Skt. Prahevajra), the first human vidyādhara (knowledge holder) of Dzogchen, traditionally said to have lived in Uḍḍiyāna in the centuries before the Common Era. Garab Dorje received the entire body of Dzogchen tantras directly from Vajrasattva and is considered the source of the teachings as they entered human experience.4

The Nyingma thus speaks of three lineages of transmission: the mind-direct lineage of the Victorious Ones (Samantabhadra to the buddhas), the symbolic lineage of the Vidyādharas (Vajrasattva to Garab Dorje and his successors), and the aural lineage of ordinary beings (from human master to human disciple). All three are still considered active in any authentic Dzogchen transmission today.5

The Three Classes of Dzogchen

✦ Sem-dé, Long-dé, Menngag-dé ✦

Garab Dorje’s foremost disciple, the paṇḍita Mañjuśrīmitra, is credited with classifying all the Dzogchen teachings into three great series or “classes” (sde gsum). This threefold division remains the organizing principle of the tradition to this day.6

I

Semdé

sems sde · Mind Series

Texts translated largely in the early period (7th–9th c.) by Vairotsana, Vimalamitra and Śrī Siṃgha. Critiques tantric striving and promotes effortless contemplation of the mind’s emptiness, luminosity, and inherent gnosis.

II

Longdé

klong sde · Space Series

The “Space” or “Expanse” class, emphasizing the spaciousness (klong) and emptiness (stong pa) aspect of the natural state. Widely systematized in the 11th–14th centuries.

III

Menngag-dé

man ngag sde · Pith Instruction Series

The innermost “Secret Instruction” class — the heart of the Nyingthig lineages. Divided into trekchö (cutting through to primordial purity) and tögal (direct approach to spontaneous presence).

The Longchen Nyingthig belongs to the innermost Menngag-dé, and specifically to its most esoteric cycle, the Nyingthig (snying thig) or “Heart Essence” teachings. Within this cycle are contained the essential instructions for realizing rigpa — naked awareness — which is the whole point of Dzogchen.7

❦ ❦ ❦

From India to the Land of Snows

✦ The unbroken lineage of rainbow-body masters ✦

From Garab Dorje, the Dzogchen teachings passed through a chain of Indian vidyādharas — Mañjuśrīmitra, Śrī Siṃgha, and Jñānasūtra. All four of these early Indian holders are said to have dissolved their physical forms into the rainbow body ('ja' lus) at the time of their passing.8

Early Centuries CE

Garab Dorje · Mañjuśrīmitra

Garab Dorje transmitted “the combined essence of the words, meanings, and esoteric instructions” to Mañjuśrīmitra over seventy-five years. Mañjuśrīmitra then organized the teachings into the three classes.

Mid-first millennium

Śrī Siṃgha · Jñānasūtra

Śrī Siṃgha received the entire transmission from Mañjuśrīmitra over twenty-five years, and in turn passed the pith-instruction teachings to Jñānasūtra — the last of the four human vidyādharas to attain rainbow body.

8th century CE

The Three Masters Come to Tibet

During the reign of King Trisong Detsen, three figures carry Dzogchen to Tibet: Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche), Vimalamitra, and the Tibetan translator Vairotsana. Padmasambhava and Vairotsana received their transmissions from Śrī Siṃgha; Vimalamitra received his from both Śrī Siṃgha and Jñānasūtra.

8th–9th centuries

The Two Root Heart Essences Emerge

Vimalamitra concealed his most secret pith instructions as terma, later revealed as the Vima Nyingthig. Padmasambhava transmitted a parallel set of teachings to his consort Yeshe Tsogyal and Princess Pemasal, later revealed as the Khandro Nyingthig (“Heart Essence of the Ṣākinīs”).

These two lineages — the scholar’s Vima Nyingthig and the yogini’s Khandro Nyingthig — would remain the two great streams of Nyingthig transmission in Tibet for the next five centuries, until they were united by one remarkable figure in the 14th century.9

❦ ❦ ❦

Longchenpa — The Great Systematizer

✦ Drimed Özer · 1308–1364 ✦

The figure who would give the Nyingthig tradition its definitive form was Longchen Rabjam (Klong chen rab 'byams), better known simply as Longchenpa. A scholar-yogi of extraordinary range, he composed more than two hundred works, most famously his Seven Treasuries (mdzod bdun) and the Nyingthig Yabzhi (snying thig ya bzhi), a compilation of commentaries that unified and clarified the Vima Nyingthig and Khandro Nyingthig cycles.10

Longchenpa is credited with the scholastic systematization of Dzogchen thought within the broader Tibetan Vajrayana — a feat that is widely regarded as the reason the Longchen Nyingthig lineage came to predominate over other Dzogchen traditions in later centuries.11

The Seven Treasuries (mdzod bdun)

Wish-Fulfilling

A systematic presentation of the path of sutra and tantra, with auto-commentary.

Pith Instructions

Essential Dzogchen instructions organized as practical advice for meditation.

Basic Space of Phenomena

The philosophical heart of Dzogchen view, explaining the dharmadhātu.

Philosophical Tenets

A presentation of Buddhist and non-Buddhist philosophical schools.

Supreme Vehicle

The tantric basis of the Great Perfection view and path.

Word and Meaning

Explanation of the key terms and their experiential meaning in Dzogchen.

Natural State

The nature of reality as it is — often considered the culminating treasury.

“Longchenpa’s work led to the dominance of the Longchen Nyingthig lineage of Dzogchen over the other Dzogchen traditions.” — Wikipedia, citing Dudjom Rinpoche’s School of the Ancients

Though Longchenpa did not write a text called “Longchen Nyingthig” — that title belongs to Jigme Lingpa’s terma — his synthesis of the two earlier Nyingthigs provided the doctrinal and textual foundation on which Jigme Lingpa’s revelation would rest.

❦ ❦ ❦

Jigme Lingpa — The Vast Expanse Revealed

✦ Khyentse Özer · 1730–1798 ✦

In the summer of 1757, a 27-year-old practitioner named Jigme Lingpa entered a three-year retreat at Palri in central Tibet. During this retreat he received three visions of Longchen Rabjam — who had died nearly four centuries earlier. In these visions, Longchenpa blessed him with his body, speech, and mind, and transmitted the Longchen Nyingthig.12

Tradition holds that Jigme Lingpa was simultaneously an incarnation of King Trisong Detsen (who had invited Padmasambhava to Tibet) and of Vimalamitra himself. Through him, therefore, Tibet’s two primary Dzogchen lineages — the Vima Nyingthig and the Khandro Nyingthig — were re-united in a single tertön (treasure-revealer).13

Jigme Lingpa kept his revelation secret for seven years. Then, in 1764, he gave the first empowerments to a group of fifteen disciples. After that, he established a hermitage at Tsering Jong in the Long-Life Valley of central Tibet, and the teachings spread rapidly. His two foremost disciples — the First Dodrupchen Jigme Trinle Özer and Jigme Gyalwai Nyugu — carried the Longchen Nyingthig throughout Tibet, and especially into the eastern region of Kham.14

“Longchen Nyingthig is the essence or embodiment of the two Nyingthig traditions, Vima Nyingthig and Khandro Nyingthig.” — Rigpa Wiki

The Three Incarnations of Jigme Lingpa

After his passing, Jigme Lingpa was said to have manifested simultaneously in three emanations — a pattern unusual even in the tertön tradition. Each became a pillar of the subsequent Longchen Nyingthig lineage:15

Do Khyentse Yeshe Dorje

1800–1866 · Body Incarnation

A wandering yogi whose unconventional realization influenced generations of disciples across eastern Tibet.

Patrul Rinpoche

1808–1887 · Speech Incarnation

Jigme Chokyi Wangpo — the legendary “enlightened vagabond” whose Kunzang Lamai Shelung (“Words of My Perfect Teacher”) remains the classic practice manual for the Longchen Nyingthig ngöndro.

Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo

1820–1892 · Mind Incarnation

Co-founder of the non-sectarian Rimé movement, who gathered the surviving teachings of all schools and passed the Longchen Nyingthig to a new generation of masters.

The Path of Practice

✦ From foundation to the Great Perfection ✦

The Longchen Nyingthig offers a complete path, structured so that the pinnacle teachings rest on a solid foundation of preliminary practice, devotion, and realization of emptiness. The tradition is emphatic that the advanced practices of Dzogchen cannot be safely or fruitfully undertaken without completing what comes before.16

1. The Preliminary Practices — Ngöndro

Before approaching Dzogchen proper, a practitioner completes the ngöndro, a graduated set of foundational practices designed to purify obscurations and accumulate merit. The Longchen Nyingthig ngöndro has five principal inner sections: refuge, bodhicitta, Vajrasattva purification, maṇḍala offering, and guru yoga. Each is typically performed 100,000 times.17

Patrul Rinpoche’s celebrated commentary, Kunzang Lamai Shelung (“The Words of My Perfect Teacher”), is the beloved guide for this stage. Patrul wrote it as a direct record of the oral teachings he had received from his own master, Jigme Gyalwai Nyugu, and it has been a primary reference for Nyingma practitioners for nearly two centuries.18

2. The Main Sādhana Practice

Following the preliminaries, practitioners engage the development (kyerim) and completion (dzogrim) stages of the tantric sādhanas contained in the Longchen Nyingthig cycle — most centrally the practices of the inner guru Padmasambhava, the peaceful and wrathful deities, and the protectors. This stage ripens the practitioner’s mind for the subtle recognition required by Dzogchen.

3. The Great Perfection — Dzogchen

The culminating teachings are contained in Jigme Lingpa’s masterwork, Yeshe Lama (The Wisdom Guru), which he composed as a summary of the Vima Nyingthig — the most important Dzogchen practice manual in the Nyingma tradition. It presents the two principal phases of Dzogchen practice:19

Trekchö

khregs chod · “Cutting Through”

The practice of recognizing and resting in kadag — primordial purity. One identifies the innate pure awareness (rigpa) and sustains that recognition in all activities. Trekchö is often described as “cutting through” the stubborn grip of conceptual mind to reveal the natural state beneath.

Tögal

thod rgal · “Direct Crossing”

The practice of lhündrup — spontaneous presence. Through specific postures, gazes, and engagement with light, tögal brings the direct realization of the three kāyas in this very life. It is undertaken only after stability in trekchö has been achieved.

“The empty quality of mind-essence is primordial purity, while the cognizant quality is spontaneous presence. Their inseparability is the ultimate path in Dzogchen.” — paraphrased from Rigpa Wiki / Dilgo Khyentse Rinpoche

The fruit of these practices, according to tradition, is the “body of light” or rainbow body ('ja' lus) — the dissolution of the coarse physical elements into pure luminous awareness at the moment of death. This phenomenon, attested in the biographies of the first four vidyādharas and reported as recently as the 20th century among Nyingma masters, is considered the ultimate sign of accomplishment in the Great Perfection.

The Living Lineage

✦ 19th – 21st century masters ✦

The Longchen Nyingthig has never stopped being a living tradition. From Patrul Rinpoche’s generation in the 19th century, the lineage flowed through a remarkable succession of masters whose teachings shaped modern Tibetan Buddhism — and, increasingly, modern global Buddhism.20

Nyoshul Lungtok Tenpe Nyima

1829–1901/2

Principal Dzogchen student of Patrul Rinpoche, and the primary transmitter of the Nyingthig practice lineage to the next generation of hermits and scholars.

Adzom Drukpa Drodul Pawo Dorje

1842–1924

A disciple of both Patrul Rinpoche and Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo, he served as a major terton and teacher, and as Dzongsar Khyentse’s principal Dzogchen master.

Dudjom Rinpoche

1904–1987

Jigdral Yeshe Dorje — the supreme head of the Nyingma school from the Chinese invasion of Tibet until his death. Author of the encyclopedic The Nyingma School of Tibetan Buddhism: Its Fundamentals and History.

Dilgo Khyentse Rinpoche

1910–1991

A principal holder of the Longchen Nyingthig, head of the Nyingma school after Dudjom Rinpoche, and the 14th Dalai Lama’s “main teacher of Dzogchen.” Spiritual advisor to the royal family of Bhutan.

Nyoshul Khen Rinpoche

1932–1999

Jamyang Dorje — an itinerant Dzogchen master described as one of the few contemporary lamas from whom nearly every other contemporary Dzogchen teacher received instruction.

Khenchen Jigme Phuntsok

1933–2004

Founder of Larung Gar in Serthar — the largest Buddhist study institute in the world — and the most influential Nyingma lama in contemporary Tibet. A major force in the revival of Dzogchen study and practice after the Cultural Revolution.

Today, the Longchen Nyingthig is taught and practiced at dozens of monasteries and retreat centers across Tibet, Bhutan, Nepal, India, and the global diaspora. Contemporary holders include Kyabgön Dodrupchen Rinpoche, Kyabjé Trulshik Rinpoche’s successors, the Shechen and Dzongsar lineages, and teachers such as Sogyal Rinpoche (whose Tibetan Book of Living and Dying brought elements of the lineage to millions of readers), Tsoknyi Rinpoche, Mingyur Rinpoche, and Orgyen Tobgyal Rinpoche. The non-sectarian Rimé spirit of Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo continues to shape its transmission.21

❦ ❦ ❦

A Single Current

✦ From Samantabhadra to the present day ✦

What makes the Longchen Nyingthig remarkable among Tibetan traditions is the way it holds together historical depth and immediate accessibility. Its text is relatively new — revealed only in 1757 — but its content claims a continuous transmission stretching back to the primordial Buddha himself. For its practitioners, the lineage is not a historical artifact but a living current: each empowerment, each reading transmission, each moment of introduction to the nature of mind is said to carry the same blessing that Samantabhadra transmitted to Vajrasattva at the dawn of time.

Whether one approaches it as a scholar, a practitioner, or simply a curious observer, the Longchen Nyingthig offers a window into one of the most philosophically sophisticated and experientially direct of all the world’s contemplative traditions — a tradition that, by its own account, points to what has never been other than who we already are.
